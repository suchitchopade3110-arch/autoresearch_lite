## Wave 5 - council audit hardening

A three-auditor blind council review (`responses.md`/`autoresearch_lite_council_audit.md`)
found that the eval-integrity and merge-safety invariants Wave 1/3 believed
closed did not survive an adversarial candidate diff. Every finding below
was independently re-verified against this codebase before being fixed.

## Wave 7 - independent 3-auditor council review (6/10 verdict)

An independently commissioned 3-auditor blind council (systems/engineering,
security/threat-model, and ML-research-value lenses, cross-ranked by two
blind judges) reviewed the system-design audit doc rather than the raw
codebase, and scored the design 6/10. Every finding below was
independently re-verified against this codebase - not taken on the
council's word - before being fixed or, where the finding described a
genuine documentation ambiguity rather than a code defect, corrected in
place.

| # | Finding | Verdict on re-verification | Fix | Proving test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Top-ranked by both judges: the audit doc's own wording ("§4.5 says `validate_and_apply_patch()` is the single choke point... §8 says evolutionary mode bypasses it") read as though evolutionary mode skips the diff-scope guard entirely, which combined with `target.repo_path` defaulting to `.` would let a diff reach the harness's own repo | **Not a code defect** - re-verified against `evolution/scheduler.py:207` and `evolution/population.py:105,172`: every evolutionary-mode call site already passes `allowed_files=["candidate_script.py"]` into `validate_and_apply_patch()`, which is where `vcs/diff_guard.py` is actually wired in.
